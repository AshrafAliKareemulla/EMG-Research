● Briefing

  Qwen3.6-35B-A3B — real, but wrong fit for us

  Released 2026-04-16 (yesterday). Confirmed: 35B total / 3B active MoE, 256 experts, Apache 2.0, hybrid Gated-DeltaNet
  + Gated-Attention architecture, native BF16.

  Why it's a no-go for our Quadro RTX 6000:
  - BF16-native on Turing sm_75 = fp32 emulation → slow and numerically unstable
  - Kernels for Gated-DeltaNet/Gated-Attention are brand new; vLLM/SGLang Turing support for this architecture is
  untested
  - LoRA VRAM math: 35B × 4-bit ≈ 17.5GB weights alone; training activations across MoE experts + optimizer + LoRA
  pushes realistic need to 28–40GB. Won't fit on 24GB for fine-tuning.
  - 4-bit inference might fit but isn't proven on Turing for this architecture

  TriAttention (arXiv 2604.04921, Song Han MIT + NVIDIA, Apr 2026) — real, but irrelevant for training

  The "32B on 24GB" line is marketing slippage. What TriAttention actually does: KV-cache compression for long-context
  inference only (2.5× throughput, 10.7× KV memory reduction on 32K-token reasoning). It does not shrink weights, does
  not help LoRA fine-tuning, and for T2S prompts (~2K tokens) KV cache isn't our bottleneck anyway. Also, its fast path
  likely wants FA2 (Ampere+), so Turing compatibility is questionable. Skip.

  The actual game-changer: Arctic-Text2SQL-R1-7B (Snowflake, Apache 2.0)

  We already cited this in state.md as "64× H100-trained SOTA 68.9% BIRD" — but we ruled it out because we assumed it
  meant training GRPO ourselves. We missed that Snowflake released the weights.
